What Is Private Personal Training?

What it means, how it differs from a big-box gym, and who it is for.

By Coach Dan Figueroa, ACSM certified personal trainer · Precision Nutrition Level 1 coach · Updated Nov 24, 2026

On this page

  1. The short answer
  2. How it compares
  3. Who it suits

Private personal training is one-on-one coaching that takes place by appointment in a private facility, rather than on the open floor of a public gym. You train with a coach who is working only with you, in a space without a crowd, waiting for equipment, or anyone watching. It is the most focused way to train, and for people who value their time and their privacy, it is often the one that finally sticks.

How private training compares

There are four common ways to train with help, and they are genuinely different:

  • Big-box gym with a trainer. One-on-one coaching, but on a busy public floor. You share equipment and space with everyone there that hour.
  • Semi-private training. A coach working with a small group, usually two to four people, each on their own program. More affordable, less individual attention.
  • Online coaching. A program and check-ins delivered remotely. Flexible and cheaper, but no one is in the room to correct a lift or adjust a weight.
  • Private personal training. One coach, one client, in a private room, by appointment. The most individual attention and the most privacy, at a higher price than the others.

Who private training suits

Private training fits people who are self-conscious about training in front of others, who have a schedule that will not tolerate waiting for a rack, who want every rep coached rather than a program handed over, or who simply train better without a crowd. It tends to suit professionals and business owners over 40, which is who Fig Performance, serving Yorba Linda, is built for.

What to expect

A good private session is not just a workout with company. It starts from an assessment and an InBody scan, runs on a program built for your body with progressive overload, and is measured with regular re-scans so you can see fat and muscle change separately. At Fig Performance that also includes nutrition coaching and recovery tools, all in a private studio.

The first consult and InBody scan are free, so you can see the space and the approach before deciding. Private one-on-one training is $60 to $90 a session with a 3-month minimum; semi-private is a lower-cost option if you are comfortable training in a small group.

FAQ

Straight answers.

What is private personal training?

It is one-on-one coaching by appointment in a private facility, rather than on the open floor of a public gym. One coach works only with you, with no crowd, no waiting for equipment, and no one watching.

What is the difference between private and semi-private training?

Private training is one coach and one client. Semi-private is one coach working with a small group, usually two to four people, each on their own program. Private gives more individual attention and privacy; semi-private costs less.

Is private personal training worth the cost?

It depends on what has stopped you before. If equipment and information were never the problem, and consistency and attention were, the privacy and one-on-one coaching are usually what makes training finally stick. If you will train reliably on your own, a cheaper option may fit better.

Is private training better for beginners?

Often, yes. Beginners benefit most from having every lift coached and from not feeling watched while they learn. A private setting removes the intimidation that keeps a lot of people from starting.
