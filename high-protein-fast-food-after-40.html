High-Protein Fast Food Meals for Adults Over 40

The short version

  • Find the protein first: chicken, fish, beef, eggs, tofu or beans.
  • Add fruit or vegetables when the restaurant has them.
  • Choose portions based on your needs. Check the restaurant's published nutrition information when you want exact numbers.

The one rule I use for fast food

Start by choosing a meal with a clear protein source. Then think about what else makes it satisfying, like potatoes, rice, beans, whole grains, fruit or vegetables. The amount you need depends on your body size, training and goals. There is no universal order that works for everybody.

Be careful with lists that promise a certain number of grams of protein at every chain. Menus, recipes and portions change. The restaurant's current nutrition guide is a better source for exact calories and macros. If you're choosing between two meals, compare them directly instead of relying on a social media graphic.

At a burger restaurant

A burger with a substantial meat patty can be a practical protein choice. If you prefer chicken, grilled options may be available, depending on the chain. You can keep the bun and still make progress with a fat-loss plan. Pay attention to the size of the full meal, including fries, drinks, sauces and any extra patties.

Easy order: a burger or chicken sandwich, a side you actually want, and a drink that fits your preferences. If your goal is to keep the meal lighter, consider a smaller side or skip a second sandwich. If you're trying to gain muscle and need a larger meal, a higher-protein portion can be reasonable.

At a Mexican restaurant or burrito shop

This is one of the easier places to put together a balanced meal because the ingredients are usually visible. A bowl with chicken or steak, rice, beans, vegetables and salsa covers a lot of bases. A burrito with similar fillings can work too. The tortilla is part of the meal, not something you must eliminate.

Easy order: a chicken or steak bowl with beans and vegetables. Add rice, cheese, guacamole or sour cream in amounts that make sense for your appetite and goals. If you want the burrito, order the burrito. Consistency over weeks matters more than any single lunch.

At a sandwich shop

Look for sandwiches with chicken, turkey, tuna, eggs or another clear protein filling. If the standard portion has very little filling, some shops let you add more. Vegetables are an easy addition. Bread choice is mostly about what you enjoy, what keeps you full and whether you need more fiber.

Easy order: a turkey or chicken sandwich with vegetables and a piece of fruit, when available. If you need more food after training, add a side rather than going hungry and snacking without a plan later.

At a coffee shop or breakfast drive-through

Breakfast is where people often end up with coffee and a pastry and then wonder why they're hungry again an hour later. If available, pair your drink with an egg sandwich, a yogurt option, or another meal with a meaningful protein source. You can still have the pastry if you want it.

Easy order: an egg-based breakfast sandwich and coffee, or Greek yogurt with fruit if the shop sells it. Protein shakes are another convenient option when the ingredient list and nutrition information work for you.

A realistic workday with no meal prep

  1. Breakfast: eggs and toast at home, or an egg sandwich while you commute.
  2. Lunch: a Mexican-style chicken bowl with beans, rice and vegetables.
  3. Afternoon: a yogurt, protein shake or other snack if you're hungry.
  4. Dinner: a sandwich, burger, or a regular dinner with protein and a vegetable.

This is an example of a practical routine, not a calorie prescription. I adjust portions and total protein to the person. At FIG PERFORMANCE, nutrition coaching works alongside strength training and progress measurements. The plan should fit the food you already eat.

Common questions

Can I lose fat while eating fast food?

Yes. Long-term energy intake and a pattern of eating you can sustain matter. Restaurant food can fit that pattern. Tracking portions and including enough protein and fiber can make it easier to stay consistent.

Should I skip carbs after 40?

No. Carbohydrates can support training and daily activity. The useful question is how much food your body needs across the day, including protein, carbohydrates, fats and fiber.

What if I eat at restaurants five days a week?

Pick two or three dependable meals that you enjoy and can order consistently. Use the restaurants' nutrition information if you track your intake. A coach can help adjust portions when your results stall.
