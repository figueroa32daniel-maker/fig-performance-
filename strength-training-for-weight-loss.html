Strength Training for Weight Loss: How It Actually Works

Why lifting, not just cardio, is what changes how your body looks.

By Coach Dan Figueroa, ACSM certified personal trainer · Precision Nutrition Level 1 coach · Updated Nov 24, 2026

On this page

  2. Why it beats cardio alone
  3. Why the scale lies
  4. What it looks like
  5. Where to start

Strength training drives weight loss by protecting your muscle while you lose weight, so that what comes off is fat rather than a mix of fat and muscle. A calorie deficit is what makes you lose weight; strength training is what decides whether you end up smaller and soft or leaner and stronger. For changing how your body actually looks, lifting does more than cardio alone.

Why it beats cardio alone

When you eat in a deficit, the body pulls energy from both fat and muscle. Cardio burns calories but does nothing to tell the body to keep its muscle, so people who only do cardio often lose weight and still look soft, because they lost muscle along with the fat. Strength training sends the opposite signal: it tells the body the muscle is needed, so the deficit comes out of fat instead. The result is the same scale weight with a very different body underneath. Muscle also keeps your metabolism higher, which makes the fat loss easier to hold.

Why the scale lies during fat loss

Because strength training can build a little muscle while you lose fat, the scale often moves slowly even as your body changes, a process called body recomposition. This is why we measure with an InBody scan rather than the scale: it shows fat mass and muscle mass as separate numbers, so a month where the scale barely moved can turn out to be a month where you lost eight pounds of fat and built two of muscle. Our guide on why the scale is not moving covers this in depth.

What it looks like

Fat loss that lasts is built on three things, none of them extreme:

  • A modest calorie deficit you can actually sustain, not a crash diet that costs you muscle.
  • Strength training two to three times a week, built on compound lifts and progressive overload.
  • Enough protein, usually around your goal bodyweight in grams a day, which protects muscle and keeps you full.

Daily walking on top of that does more for fat loss over time than punishing cardio sessions.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Does strength training help with weight loss?

Yes, in a specific way. A calorie deficit is what makes you lose weight, and strength training is what makes the weight you lose come from fat instead of muscle. That is the difference between ending up smaller and soft or leaner and stronger.

Is strength training or cardio better for fat loss?

Strength training does more to change how your body looks, because it protects muscle in a deficit so you lose fat rather than muscle, and it keeps metabolism higher. Cardio and daily walking help burn calories on top of that. The best fat-loss plans use both, with lifting at the core.

Why is the scale not moving even though I'm training?

Because strength training can build muscle while you lose fat, so the scale moves slowly even as your body changes shape. This is called body recomposition. A body-composition scan shows fat and muscle as separate numbers, which the scale cannot.

How much protein do I need for fat loss?

A common target is your goal bodyweight in grams of protein a day. Enough protein protects muscle while you are in a calorie deficit and keeps you fuller, which makes the deficit easier to hold.
