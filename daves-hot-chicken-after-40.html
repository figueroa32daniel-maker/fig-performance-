Unpopular opinion: one tender at Dave's Hot Chicken is a meal, not a snack.

490 calories and 25 grams of protein, from their own nutrition table.

One tender at Dave's Hot Chicken is 490 calories. Not 150, which is what several nutrition apps will tell you. Four hundred and ninety, from the chain's own published nutrition table, with 25 grams of protein and 1,210 mg of sodium. If you have been logging these as a snack, you have been undercounting by about three hundred calories per tender.

What a tender actually costs

Straight from Dave's Hot Chicken's own nutrition information:

  • Single Tender, Not Hot or Lite Mild: 490 calories, 25 g protein, 29 g fat, 1,210 mg sodium
  • Single Tender, Mild through Reaper: 550 calories, 25 g protein, 35 g fat, 1,480 mg sodium
  • Single Slider, Not Hot or Lite Mild: 620 calories, 31 g protein, 1,150 mg sodium
  • Single Slider, Mild through Reaper: 680 calories, 31 g protein, 1,420 mg sodium

Twenty-five grams of protein is fine. Four hundred and ninety calories to get it is not, and that is the honest summary of this restaurant: the protein is real and it is expensive.

The heat level changes the numbers

This is the part nobody mentions and it explains why every nutrition app disagrees about this place. Going from Not Hot to Mild or above adds about 60 calories and roughly 270 mg of sodium per tender, because the heat comes from oil and seasoning applied to the chicken.

Order four Reaper tenders instead of four Not Hot and you have added around 240 calories and over a thousand milligrams of sodium without ordering anything extra.

The heat is made of oil. Every step up the spice board costs you about sixty calories a tender.

Dave's Sauce is not a condiment

  • Dave's Sauce, 1.5 oz dipper cup: 290 calories, 30 g fat, 0 g protein
  • Dave's Sauce, 4 oz cup: 760 calories, 80 g fat
  • Ranch, 1.5 oz dipper: 200 calories

A small cup of Dave's Sauce carries more calories than two-thirds of a tender and gives you nothing in return. The large cup is 760 calories, which is more than most entire meals in these guides.

Separately, the Spicy Buffalo sauce is only 25 calories but carries around 1,180 mg of sodium in a 1.5 oz cup, which is a different kind of problem.

The sides, including the one that looks healthy

  • Kale Slaw: 270 calories, 2 g protein, 26 g fat, 170 mg sodium
  • Mac and Cheese: 290 calories, 14 g protein, 1,090 mg sodium
  • French Fries: 440 calories, 6 g protein, 1,200 mg sodium

Read the slaw again. It is kale, so it reads as the virtuous choice, and it carries 26 grams of fat for 2 grams of protein. The mac and cheese has seven times the protein for twenty more calories. That is not an argument for the mac, it is an argument for reading the card.

The top-loaded items are in a category of their own

These are the largest single numbers anywhere in these twenty-eight restaurant guides:

  • Top-Loaded Fries, Large: 1,630 calories, 51 g protein, 4,570 mg sodium
  • Top-Loaded Buffalo Mac, Large: 2,960 calories, 66 g protein, 220 g fat, 7,020 mg sodium

Seven thousand milligrams is three days of sodium in one item. I am not going to tell you never to eat it. I am going to tell you that it exists, that it is shareable, and that nothing else on this website comes within half of it.

What I would order

  1. Two tenders, Not Hot or Lite Mild, no sauce cup. Around 980 calories and 50 g of protein. That is the honest best case here.
  2. Stay at the lower spice levels if calories matter. Each step up is roughly 60 calories a tender.
  3. Mac and cheese over kale slaw if you want a side with protein in it.
  4. Skip Dave's Sauce or split one small cup. 290 calories for zero protein.

The 15-second version

If you remember one thing

  • One tender is 490 calories, not 150
  • Hotter spice adds about 60 cal and 270 mg sodium per tender
  • A small Dave's Sauce cup is 290 calories

Why this one is on the list

Because this was the hardest restaurant in the whole series to write honestly, and the reason is instructive. The pages ranking for "Dave's Hot Chicken nutrition" report a single tender at anywhere from 150 to 550 calories. People are planning meals off numbers that are wrong by a factor of three.

Dave's Hot Chicken publishes the real figures. Almost nobody reads them. This is good food that costs what it costs, and you deserve to know the actual number before you decide.

A note on these numbers

Every figure above comes from Dave's Hot Chicken's own published nutrition information, not from a third-party database. That matters more here than anywhere else in these guides: independent sites report a single tender at 150, 180, 290, 490 and 550 calories, a nearly fourfold spread, and the low figures are simply wrong. Part of the confusion is that spice level genuinely changes the numbers, so a single "tender" has two correct answers depending on how hot you ordered it. Two rows on the chain's table were not used here: the Hot Mozz items appear to be missing a column, and the small Top-Loaded Buffalo Mac lists a calories-from-fat figure that does not reconcile with its own total fat.
